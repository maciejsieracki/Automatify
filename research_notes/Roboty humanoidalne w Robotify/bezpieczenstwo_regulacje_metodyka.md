## 1. Safety standards: ISO 10218-1/-2:2025, ISO/TS 15066, ISO 25785-1, ISO 13482, ISO 3691-4, functional safety (IEC 61508 / IEC 62061 / ISO 13849), ANSI/A3 work, and what is still missing for humanoids

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 there is **no published safety standard for humanoid (dynamically stable) robots**. ISO 25785-1 is still a **draft (ISO/CD stage)**, and the A3 technical report TR R15.108 is still being drafted. Deployments therefore rest on ISO 10218-1/-2:2025, which absorbed ISO/TS 15066, applied by analogy, together with the OEM's risk file and a site-specific risk assessment. The main technical gap is the **"fail-passive gap"**: standard safe stops that cut power would make a balancing robot fall. No humanoid has published third-party safety certification for cooperative work near people. Agility's Digit 5, announced in September 2026, targets certification with shipments in 2027.

**ISO 25785-1 (DRAFT, not published)**
- ISO's catalogue lists **ISO/CD 25785-1**, "Robotics — Safety requirements for dynamically stable industrial mobile robots (legged, wheeled, or other forms of locomotion) — Part 1: Robots", as under development with a committee draft under review. Some secondary sources call it a "Working Draft", so stage labels are inconsistent. No official publication date was found. — [ISO catalogue entry](https://www.iso.org/standard/91469.html); [i-scoop explainer](https://www.i-scoop.eu/iso-25785-1-explained-and-what-it-means-for-humanoid-robot-safety/); [There's A Robot For That](https://theresarobotforthat.com/blog/humanoid-robot-safety-standards-2026/)

Examples include quadrupedal, bipedal and wheeled balancing robots. Residential and consumer settings are excluded. It is planned as multi-part: **Part 1 covers the robot, and Part 2 (to be developed separately) covers integration of applications.** — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/91469.html); [Synapticon news on ISO 25785](https://www.synapticon.com/en/newslist/iso-25785-sicherheit-dynamisch-stabile-roboter); [RoboticsBiz](https://roboticsbiz.com/iso-safety-standards-for-humanoid-robots-what-manufacturers-need-to-know-in-2026/)

These are projections, not confirmed dates. — [biped.news](https://biped.news/article/humanoid-robot-safety-standards-2026); [Tech Briefs](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/53111-safety-in-motion-setting-the-standard-for-humanoid-robots)
- ISO/TC 299 **WG 12**, "safety of dynamically stable industrial mobile robots (legged, wheeled or other forms of locomotion)", had meetings scheduled for September 2026. **ISO 10218-1:2025 / ISO 10218-2:2025 (PUBLISHED) and ISO/TS 15066**
- Both parts were published in **February 2025**. One vendor-hosted article gives 1 April 2025 as the date they "came into force", so the effective dates in sources differ. — [A3 "Updated ISO 10218 FAQ"](https://www.automate.org/robotics/blogs/updated-iso-10218-faq); [SICK blog](https://www.sick.com/br/en/more-safety-for-humans-and-machines/w/blog-robotic-norm-iso10218)
- **ISO/TS 15066:2016 no longer exists as a standalone technical specification.** Its power-and-force-limiting (PFL) and collaborative-application requirements were folded into the revised ISO 10218 series. The four collaboration techniques are unchanged but are now specified in the 2025 parts. — [EVS International](https://www.evsint.com/ro/collaborative-robot-safety-standards-2026-iso-10218-2025-ts-15066/); [There's A Robot For That](https://theresarobotforthat.com/?p=1011)
- The terms "collaborative robot" and "collaborative operation" are gone; the standard uses "**collaborative application**". Part 2 shifts emphasis from the "robot system" to the "**robot application**", and collaboration becomes a property of the application rather than of the robot. — [A3 FAQ](https://www.automate.org/robotics/blogs/updated-iso-10218-faq); [ibf-solutions](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-standards-for-industrial-robots-en-iso-10218-1-and-2)
- Other changes: Part 1 introduces **robot classes** with matching functional-safety requirements. **Cybersecurity requirements related to robot safety are new.** Required performance levels are now risk-driven rather than a blanket PL d. — [SICK](https://www.sick.com/br/en/more-safety-for-humans-and-machines/w/blog-robotic-norm-iso10218); [ibf-solutions](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-standards-for-industrial-robots-en-iso-10218-1-and-2)

**Functional safety and the "fail-passive gap" (core missing piece for humanoids)**
- An arXiv preprint (Ding, Cui, Wang, Wen; 2026), "Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots: The Fail-Passive Gap and a Feasibility Study", argues that **ISO 13849-1 and EN 60204-1 assume fail-passive design**, while a legged robot's safe state must be actively controlled. Cutting power to a walking biped makes it fall, so de-energising is itself a hazard. — [arXiv 2608.02809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02809)
- The same paper: the certifiable reaction element in a reference Siemens S7-1500 safety chain is contactor-based power removal (**Stop Category 0**), which a balancing humanoid cannot use. The authors isolate the uncertifiable robot-side reaction chain behind a certified external chain (light curtain, E-stop, fail-safe PLC, wireless PROFIsafe) in a 3 m × 1.5 m semi-enclosed cell with a Unitree G1. They **explicitly do not claim PL e / SIL 3 end-to-end**. They note the robot's onboard compute is not safety-rated, and they also cover fall-as-hazard analysis and ISO 13855 separation distances. — [arXiv 2608.02809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02809)
- Synapticon's white paper (vendor): classic Safe Torque Off "does not lead to a safe stop" in a two-legged robot. — [Synapticon white paper "Safety for humanoid robots"](https://www.synapticon.com/en/newslist/whitepaper-safety-fur-humanoide-roboter); [Synapticon POSITRON white paper](https://www.synapticon.com/en/newslist/whitepaper-positron-safety-humanoide-roboter)
- Synapticon and Reynolds & Moore announced a partnership "to close the safety gap in humanoid robotics" (A3 news item). — [A3 news](https://www.automate.org/news/synapticon-and-reynolds-and-moore-partner-to-close-the-safety-gap-in-humanoid-robotics)
- The Danish Technological Institute offers **type examination of machines that use AI for safety functions**. This is an early example of notified-body-style services aimed at AI safety functions. — [DTI](https://www.dti.dk/services/type-examination-of-machines-that-use-ai-for-safety-functions/47581)

### Inferences
- For a Polish integrator in 2026–2027, the defensible safety basis for a humanoid application is: (1) ISO 12100 risk assessment of the specific application; (2) ISO 10218-2:2025 applied by analogy to the "robot application", including its collaborative-application and PFL content (formerly ISO/TS 15066); (3) ISO 13849-1 / IEC 62061 for the external safety functions (zone scanners, light curtains, E-stop, safety PLC); (4) the OEM's risk file and safety manual; (5) "watching brief" alignment with ISO/CD 25785-1 and TR R15.108 drafts. The practical consequence is **spatial or temporal separation** (fenced or zoned cells, speed and separation monitoring with external safety-rated sensors). True fence-less work near people is not yet certifiable against a published humanoid standard.
- Because humanoids cannot safely use STO / Stop Category 0 while balancing, the risk assessment must treat **falling (60–70+ kg mass, toppling envelope) as a hazard**. It must define a "controlled sit/crouch" or "pre-stop to stable stance" behaviour (a Stop Category 1/2-like concept), and size separation distances (ISO 13855) to include the fall envelope. This is the area where ISO 25785-1 is expected to give guidance (inference from the scope and the fail-passive analysis).
- Cobots remain on mature, published ground (ISO 10218-1/-2:2025 with integrated PFL limits). This is a strong argument for keeping close human-contact tasks on cobots during the transition period.

## 2. EU and Polish law: Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, AI Act, Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853, GDPR, CE responsibilities when an integrator modifies or combines robots, Polish specifics (PIP, UDT, OHS, worker consultation)

### Takeaway
From **20 January 2027** the Machinery Regulation (MR) replaces the Machinery Directive with no parallel transition period. It adds cybersecurity ("protection against corruption") and self-evolving-behaviour requirements, and puts ML-based safety components under notified-body assessment. The **AI Act omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force 27 July 2026), moved machinery to Annex I Section B**. AI-specific requirements for machinery will therefore come via the MR (delegated acts), not directly from the AI Act, and Annex I high-risk dates slip to 2 August 2028. The new **PLD applies to products placed on the market after 8 December 2026**. It covers software and AI, and makes anyone who substantially modifies a product liable as manufacturer. An integrator or RaaS provider that combines or substantially modifies robots becomes the "manufacturer" under both the MR and the PLD. In Poland, a new AI law (Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003) set up the KRiBSI supervisory commission. UDT oversight applies to autonomous trucks with mechanical lifting. Robot-mounted cameras fall under art. 22² of the Labour Code and GDPR.

### Cited Findings

**Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230: timing and scope**
- In force since July 2023; most provisions apply from **20 January 2027**, repealing Directive 2006/42/EC. Machinery placed on the market before that date must comply with the Directive and may continue to be made available. — [Baker McKenzie Machinery Regulation briefing (PDF)](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/12/Baker-McKenzie-Machinery-Regulation.pdf); [Baker McKenzie Product Risk Radar (Nov 2025)](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/resources/product-risk-radar-articles/machinery-regulation); [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/regulation-eu-2023-1230-on-machinery-a-legal-regime-for-machinery-in-europe)
- There is no parallel transition period; the Directive stops applying on that date. — [TÜV NORD](https://www.tuev-nord.de/en/services/audit-and-expertise/product-certification/machinery-regulation/); [Fabrico](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/)
- Article 50(1) on penalties applied from 14 October 2023. — [DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/en-au/news-and-insights/insights/2023/7/new-machinery-regulation)

**MR: AI and self-evolving behaviour; conformity assessment**
- Annex I Part A (machinery requiring notified-body involvement) includes **"safety components with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour using machine learning approaches ensuring safety functions"** and **machinery with embedded systems of that kind, in respect only of those systems**. — [Baker McKenzie PDF](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/12/Baker-McKenzie-Machinery-Regulation.pdf); [CEMA](https://cema-agri.org/publication/news/1025-the-new-machinery-regulation-is-published-new-requirements-and-upcoming-steps)
- The general rule is self-assessment, but listed (Annex I) machinery must be validated through notified bodies. — [DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/fr-fr/news-and-insights/insights/2023/7/new-machinery-regulation)
- "Self-evolving behaviour" **is not officially defined**. DTI's working definition: software or an algorithm that uses machine learning and operates on generalisations derived from data. — [Danish Technological Institute](https://www.dti.dk/services/type-examination-of-machines-that-use-ai-for-safety-functions/47581)
- For machinery with fully or partially self-evolving logic, the **risk assessment must take into account the machine's behaviour after it is placed on the market**. — [Compliance & Risks](https://www.complianceandrisks.com/?p=20887)

**MR: cybersecurity**
- Annex III 1.1.9 "Protection against corruption": connecting another device, directly or remotely, must not create a hazardous situation, and safety-critical software and data must be identified and protected against accidental or intentional corruption. Annex III 1.2.1 requires control systems designed to withstand malicious third-party attacks, and reliability against hardware or logic faults. — [Compliance & Risks](https://www.complianceandrisks.com/?p=20887); [CENELEC BOSS "main changes Annex I MD → Annex III MR" (PDF)](https://boss.cenelec.eu/media/BOSS%20CENELEC/ref/machinery_main-changes_annex1_md_annex3_mr.pdf)
- The draft harmonised standard **prEN 50742** (DRAFT) is meant to give 1.1.9 concrete form, covering hardware interfaces, software and data that could affect machine safety; it also addresses 1.2.1 a) and f). Its aim is to protect safety functions from unauthorised interference, not general IT security. — [ibf-solutions (ES)](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/es/noticias-y-conocimientos/articulos-tecnicos-y-noticias-sobre-el-marcado-ce/nueva-norma-pren-50742-proteccion-contra-la-corrupcion)

**MR: substantial modification and integrator as manufacturer (CE responsibility)**
- **Art. 3(16)**: "substantial modification" is a modification by **physical or digital means** after placing on the market or putting into service, not foreseen by the manufacturer, that affects safety by creating a new hazard or increasing an existing risk so that new or modified protective measures are needed. — [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/regulation-eu-2023-1230-on-machinery-a-legal-regime-for-machinery-in-europe); [Unchained Robotics](https://blog.unchainedrobotics.de/en/eu-machinery-regulation-2027-robot-cells)
- **Art. 18**: a person who carries out a substantial modification is considered a **manufacturer**, subject to the Art. 10 obligations: carry out the relevant conformity assessment, draw up the EU declaration of conformity and affix CE marking. — [Unchained Robotics](https://blog.unchainedrobotics.de/en/eu-machinery-regulation-2027-robot-cells); [All4Pack memo](https://news.all4pack.com/?p=3784)
- Practical test: adding a robot or new station to an existing line is likely substantial (new hazard zone). A data-only sensor that cannot act on the machine is usually not. "The test is not how big the job was. The test is what it did to the risk." — [Unchained Robotics](https://blog.unchainedrobotics.de/en/eu-machinery-regulation-2027-robot-cells)
- If the manufacturer describes a modification in the instructions and defines its limits, it is "foreseen" and **not** substantial. This is a key lever for integrators when contracting with OEMs (secondary summary). — [Unchained Robotics](https://blog.unchainedrobotics.de/en/eu-machinery-regulation-2027-robot-cells)

### Inferences
- **Role mapping for a Polish integrator / RaaS provider (inference from MR Art. 3(16)/18 and the PLD):**
  - Deploying an OEM humanoid "as-is" within the OEM's intended use and instructions makes the integrator or RaaS provider at most a distributor or importer (if the OEM is outside the EU). Importer duties apply: check CE, DoC, instructions in Polish, and traceability. The *client* is the employer/user and bears the OHS user duties.
  - Building an **application or cell** (humanoid + conveyors + cobots + safety system + software), or making unforeseen hardware or software changes (new end-effector, new task policy outside OEM limits, new safety configuration), makes the integrator the **manufacturer of the assembly or of the modified machine**. That brings full conformity assessment, technical file, DoC and CE, and PLD manufacturer liability for products placed on the market after 8 Dec 2026.
  - **Pushing new AI models or skills to the fleet** can be a "digital" substantial modification under the MR and a substantial modification under the PLD if it changes safety behaviour beyond what the OEM foresaw. RaaS contracts should require the OEM to define the envelope of foreseen updates, and should define who validates each release.
- **Notified body exposure:** if any safety function relies on ML (e.g. learned person detection used to trigger a slow-down), the system falls under Annex I Part A and needs a notified body from 20 Jan 2027. A practical design rule for 2027 deployments is to keep **all safety functions on conventional, safety-rated sensors and logic** (laser scanners, safety PLC, ISO 13849 PL d) and confine AI to non-safety task execution. This keeps the conformity route as self-assessment (inference; the "self-evolving" definition is unsettled per DTI).
- **AI Act:** with machinery in Annex I Section B, the humanoid as a machinery product is not directly subject to AI Act high-risk obligations. AI-specific requirements will arrive via MR delegated acts (2027–2028). However, **fleet or task-allocation software that assigns tasks to, monitors or evaluates human workers** in a mixed human–robot team could fall under Annex III (employment), applicable from 2 Dec 2027. Some workplace uses of camera data (e.g. emotion recognition) are banned outright (see Gaps; not verified this session).
- **Cameras on humanoids** collect personal data of workers continuously. Under art. 22² KP the stated purpose must be safety or production control, not productivity monitoring of people. The employer (client) is controller, and the RaaS provider is typically processor, or a separate controller if it uses data for model training. The plan should include a DPIA, a data processing agreement (art. 28 GDPR), face blurring or minimisation before data leaves the site, and notice to workers. Teleoperation and training-data uploads to non-EU OEM clouds raise GDPR Chapter V transfer issues.
- **UDT:** a legged humanoid carrying totes is not a "wózek jezdniowy podnośnikowy", so by the UDT criterion it is likely outside technical inspection. Autonomous **forklift-type AMRs in the same mixed fleet** likely need UDT registration and periodic inspection, and wheeled humanoids with lifting torsos are an edge case to clarify with the local UDT branch (inference from the UDT "mechanical lifting drive" criterion).

## 3. Practical deployment methodology used by leading companies (Agility, Figure, Apptronik, GXO, BMW, Mercedes, UBTech, Hexagon), from site assessment to fleet operation

### Takeaway
Real deployments by October 2026 follow a consistent pattern:
- **Pilot on one narrow, repetitive, non-safety-critical task** in a zoned area: tote moving between conveyors and cobots, sheet-metal loading, sequencing parts into trolleys, line-side delivery.
- Pilots run for **months, not weeks**: BMW/Figure ran 11 months, BMW Leipzig went from first test in Dec 2025 to a summer 2026 pilot, Rossmann runs through 2026, and Amazon reportedly uses 18-month pilots.
- **Explicit KPIs** (cycle time, placement accuracy, interventions per shift).
- A **cloud fleet platform** (e.g. Agility Arc) integrated with WMS/MES/PLC.
- Commercial model: **RaaS subscriptions** that bundle software and maintenance.

Scaling evidence is still thin. Most performance numbers are vendor-reported, and analysts (Gartner) expect very few companies to get beyond pilots within two years.

### Inferences
**Step-by-step methodology for a Polish integrator / RaaS provider**, synthesised from the case studies above and the legal framework in section 2. Durations are inferred from the BMW, Rossmann, GXO and Amazon timelines and are not vendor-quoted.

1. **Discovery and task screening (2–4 weeks).** List candidate tasks and score them on: repetitiveness; payload (in practice ≤15–25 kg, per OEM spec); cycle-time tolerance (humanoids are ≤50% of human productivity per UBTech, so tasks must tolerate slower cycles); variability; floor and stairs; the existing ergonomic burden (strong for worker acceptance); and isolation from public or untrained pedestrian traffic. Good first tasks per precedent are tote handling between conveyors and AMRs, sheet-metal or part loading into fixtures, kitting and sequencing, and line-side delivery.
2. **Site assessment (1–3 weeks on site).** Map the layout, aisle widths, floor quality, lighting, Wi-Fi/5G coverage, charging or swap locations, interfaces (conveyors, PLCs, WMS/MES), and human traffic flows. Identify existing UDT-registered equipment and the client's existing OHS risk assessment.
3. **Regulatory and safety design (in parallel, 4–8 weeks).**
   - Decide the role per section 2 (distributor/importer vs. manufacturer of the assembly).
   - Run an ISO 12100 risk assessment of the application, including fall hazard and controlled stop.
   - Design zoning with ISO 13849 PL d external safety functions and decide CE responsibility for the cell.
   - For GDPR, prepare the DPIA, the processing agreement and the art. 22² KP workplace-rules update.
   - Consult employees and unions (art. 237¹¹ᵃ KP, to verify).
   - Clarify UDT status for any lifting AMRs.
4. **Simulation / digital twin (2–6 weeks).** Run a cycle-time and traffic simulation of the mixed fleet and an offline layout check. No case-study source found in this session quantifies this step for humanoids.
5. **Pilot (3–12 months).** Run 1–3 robots on one task in a zoned area with KPIs:
   - cycle time and load time vs. target;
   - placement or pick accuracy (≥99% per BMW);
   - interventions per shift (target 0);
   - MTBF/MTTR;
   - uptime and availability;
   - cost per task or per robot-hour vs. labour;
   - safety incidents and near misses;
   - worker acceptance.
   Ramp up the shift length progressively (BMW reached 10-hour shifts).
6. **Data collection and teleoperation.** Collect task demonstrations and edge cases to retrain policies, with GDPR-compliant handling of video. Teleoperation sessions are also a possible "substantial modification" or change-control event.
7. **Integration (overlapping with the pilot).** Connect WMS/WES/MES via APIs or webhooks, PLC handshakes with conveyors and cobot cells, and safety integration with the workcell E-stop. The fleet platform (e.g. Arc) handles task dispatch, charging and remote monitoring.
8. **Scale-up decision gate.** Base it on KPI evidence, total cost of ownership and safety record; Amazon's gate is about 18 months.
9. **Fleet operation.** Covers:
   - charging or battery-swap stations (AEON swaps in 23 s; Walker S2 hot-swaps);
   - a spare-parts stock weighted to known failure points (forearm or wrist per Figure);
   - remote monitoring and 2nd/3rd-line OEM support;
   - a local 1st-line technician;
   - OTA update change-control, re-validated against the risk assessment and treated as a potential MR/PLD "substantial modification";
   - operator and maintenance training;
   - an incident log.
10. **Change management.** Communicate early, involve the OHS committee and unions, and position robots on ergonomically harmful tasks (Rossmann's stated focus on ergonomics). Avoid productivity surveillance of workers, which is barred as a purpose under art. 22² KP and potentially high-risk under the AI Act.

## 4. Hybrid cobot + humanoid operations: orchestration of mixed fleets, interoperability standards (VDA 5050, MassRobotics, ROS 2), task split, migration path

### Takeaway
Mixed operation is already the norm: Digit at GXO/SPANX moves totes **between cobots and conveyors**, and Agility integrates with MiR and Zebra AMRs. However, **no interoperability standard yet addresses humanoids**:
- **VDA 5050 v3.0.0** (adopted Feb 2026) covers wheeled AGVs and AMRs, now with freely navigating path sharing and zones.
- The **MassRobotics Interoperability Standard** is an AMR status-sharing protocol.
Humanoids are therefore orchestrated through vendor fleet platforms bridged to WMS/MES/PLC. Cobots should keep fixed-station, high-precision, close-contact work, while humanoids take mobile, variable brownfield tasks.

### Cited Findings
- **VDA 5050 v3.0.0** was formally adopted by VDA and VDMA on **17 February 2026**, with legal sign-off following. It adds AMR-native features: freely navigating robots can **share self-planned paths with the fleet manager**, and a **zone concept** communicates traffic rules. — [idealworks (Mar 2026)](https://www.idealworks.com/news/vda5050-300-release-march-2026); [BlueBotics VDA 5050 explainer (2026)](https://bluebotics.com/blog/vda-5050-explained-agv-communication-standard)
- The open-source library libVDA5050++ (Open Logistics Foundation) was reported by the search summary as still implementing spec 2.1.0, so implementations may lag v3. Note that library tags v3.x exist, which are library versions, not spec versions. — [Open Logistics Foundation GitLab](https://git.openlogisticsfoundation.org/standalone-projects/vda5050/libvda5050pp/-/tags/v3.2.1)
- A promotional LinkedIn post claims v3 supports "ALL TYPES of mobile robots". This is not an official statement. — [LinkedIn](https://at.linkedin.com/in/rubenknitter)
- **MassRobotics Interoperability Standard** v1.0 (2021) lets AMRs from multiple vendors share status and operational conventions without sharing proprietary maps. It is **complementary to VDA 5050**; a robot can use both. — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/interop-massrobotics-amr-standard/); [MassRobotics / BlueBotics article](https://www.massrobotics.org/?p=4742); [ABI Research](https://www.abiresearch.com/market-research/insight/7779407-massrobotics-push-for-interoperability-pre)
- No search result showed humanoid coverage in either VDA 5050 or the MassRobotics standard. — [idealworks](https://www.idealworks.com/news/vda5050-300-release-march-2026); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/interop-massrobotics-amr-standard/)
- Live hybrid example: Digit moves totes **between cobots and conveyors** at GXO's SPANX site, managed via Agility Arc. — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/agility-robotics-digit-humanoid-lands-first-official-job/); [Agility](https://www.agilityrobotics.com/content/gxo-signs-industry-first-multi-year-agreement-with-agility-robotics)
- Digit integrates with MiR and Zebra AMRs and with MES/WMS/WES/PLC systems (secondary). — [Chozan](https://chozan.co/agility-robotics/)
- At BMW, the humanoid loads fixtures and **six-axis industrial robots then weld**. This is a humanoid-to-industrial-robot hand-off at a fixed interface. — [Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/figure-retires-the-f-02-battle-scarred-robots-30-000-bmws-and-hard-lessons-learned)
- ISO 10218-2:2025 treats collaboration as a property of the **application**. A mixed cobot + humanoid cell is assessed as one robot application. — [A3 FAQ](https://www.automate.org/robotics/blogs/updated-iso-10218-faq)

### Inferences
- **Reference architecture for a mixed fleet (inference):**
  - **Top layer:** WMS/WES/MES issue tasks.
  - **Orchestration layer:** a site-level "robot orchestrator" or WES dispatches to (a) a VDA 5050 master control for AGVs/AMRs and wheeled bases, (b) the humanoid OEM fleet platform (e.g. Arc) via REST/webhooks, and (c) cobot cells via PLC/OPC UA handshakes.
  - **Safety layer:** separate and hard-wired. Zone scanners and a safety PLC define human, robot and shared zones. Shared zones apply ISO 10218-2:2025 collaborative-application rules for cobots, while humanoids operate only in human-restricted or speed-and-separation-monitored zones until ISO 25785-1 is published.
- **Task split (inference):**
  - *Keep on cobots:* fixed-station, high-cadence, high-precision tasks (screwdriving, dispensing, machine tending at one station, end-of-line palletising, inspection with fixed cameras), and tasks requiring close or contact collaboration with workers, where PFL is standardised.
  - *Move to humanoids:* tasks needing mobility across stations in brownfield layouts designed for people (tote transfer between conveyors, racks and AMRs; line-side replenishment; kitting and sequencing into trolleys; loading fixtures from racks), multi-task flexing between stations, and tasks where installing fixed automation is not economic.
  - *Keep on AMRs/AGVs:* long-distance horizontal transport. VDA 5050 v3 now supports free navigation.
- **Migration path (inference):**
  1. Cobots at fixed cells plus AMRs, with the humanoid as a "mobile link" between them (the GXO pattern).
  2. The humanoid takes over several manual hand-off points in one zoned area.
  3. After ISO 25785-1 and OEM certification (Digit 5 targets 2027 shipments), extend humanoids into shared zones with fewer barriers.
  4. Re-plan cobot cells that remain under-utilised, and redeploy cobots to tasks where precision matters.
  Each step changes the "application", so each needs an updated risk assessment and possibly a new DoC.
- Lack of a humanoid-capable open protocol means **vendor lock-in risk** for a RaaS provider. Contractually require API access to the fleet platform, and prefer OEMs that expose VDA 5050 for wheeled bases.

### Gaps
- **ROS 2 / Open-RMF** use in commercial humanoid fleets was not researched; the search budget was exhausted.
- No source on **OPC UA companion specs** for robotics (OPC 40010) in humanoid integration.
- Whether VDA 5050 v3 or any VDMA/MassRobotics initiative plans **humanoid profiles** is unknown.
- No case study quantifying productivity of mixed cobot + humanoid cells was found.

## 5. Typical deployment timelines and cost components

### Takeaway
Observed timelines run **months to over a year from first test to productive pilot**: BMW Leipzig went from first test in Dec 2025 to production tasks by June 2026; Figure at BMW ran 11 months; Rossmann's pilot runs a full year; GXO went from a late-2023 pilot to a multi-year contract. Amazon reportedly gates scale-up after **18 months**. Costs are dominated by RaaS subscriptions (a publicly cited illustrative ~$8.5k/month per Digit, unverified), integration engineering, safety infrastructure, charging and swap infrastructure, and support. Hardware unit prices were reported at >$100k with forecasts toward $20k. Hard, verified cost breakdowns are scarce.

### Cited Findings
- BMW Leipzig / Hexagon AEON: first test **Dec 2025** → further test **Apr 2026** → pilot phase **summer 2026**; production tasks reported begun by **12 Jun 2026**. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/bmw-group-brings-humanoid-robots-to-germany); [Hexagon](https://robotics.hexagon.com/bmw-deploys-aeon-hexagon-robotics-humanoid/)
- Figure 02 at BMW Spartanburg: **11 months**, 1,250+ hours, ending at 10-hour shifts Mon–Fri. — [Humanoids Daily](https://www.humanoidsdaily.com/news/figure-retires-the-f-02-battle-scarred-robots-30-000-bmws-and-hard-lessons-learned)
- GXO: pilot in **late 2023** → multi-year RaaS agreement (announced 2024). — [GXO](https://gxo.com/news_article/gxo-signs-industry-first-multi-year-agreement-with-agility-robotics/)
- Rossmann: pilot runs **through 2026** (about 12 months). — [Commodity Board](https://commodity-board.com/rossmann-pilots-humanoid-walker-s2-robot-in-german-logistics-hub-signalling-next-phase-of-warehouse-automation)
- Amazon: **18-month pilot** before scaling decisions (IDTechEx via AJOT). — [AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/fast-growth-of-humanoid-robots-in-the-automotive-and-logistics-industry)

- Schaeffler/Hexagon: ≥1,000 humanoids over **7 years** after the pilot, indicating multi-year scale-up horizons. — [Hexagon customer stories](https://robotics.hexagon.com/category/news/customer-stories/)
- Unit prices "often exceeding **US$100,000** per unit", with a forecast decline toward **US$20,000** (IDTechEx). — [AJOT / IDTechEx](https://www.ajot.com/news/fast-growth-of-humanoid-robots-in-the-automotive-and-logistics-industry)

- Integration is often the bottleneck: humanoids are only as useful as the WMS integration and task-assignment software they connect to. — [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/humanoid-robots-enter-commercial-workplaces-what-enterprises-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- **Indicative timeline for a Polish RaaS deployment (inference from the precedents):**
  - discovery and site assessment: 1–2 months;
  - legal, safety design, consultation and DPIA: 1–3 months, in parallel;
  - cell build, integration and FAT/SAT: 1–3 months;
  - supervised pilot: 3–12 months;
  - scale-up decision at about 12–18 months from kickoff.
  End-to-end, the first productive robot is realistic in about 4–6 months, and fleet scale in more than 12 months.
- **Cost components to model:**
  1. Robot hardware or RaaS fee (per robot per month or hour, including OEM software and maintenance).
  2. Fleet platform licences.
  3. Integration engineering: WMS/MES/PLC interfaces, end-effectors, task programming or policy training, including teleoperation data collection.
  4. Safety infrastructure: scanners, light curtains, fencing or zoning, safety PLC, validation.
  5. CE/conformity work: risk assessment, technical file, notified body if ML safety functions are used, legal review.
  6. Charging or battery-swap stations and spare batteries.
  7. Site works: Wi-Fi/5G, floor marking, lighting.
  8. Spare parts stock (wrist and forearm assemblies, sensors).
  9. On-site technician and remote monitoring (24/7 NOC).
  10. Training: operators, maintenance, OHS staff.
  11. Insurance: product and third-party liability, reflecting PLD manufacturer exposure from Dec 2026.
  12. Change management and consultation.
  13. A productivity buffer: humanoids at ≤50% of human throughput imply 2+ robots per replaced FTE-shift today.
