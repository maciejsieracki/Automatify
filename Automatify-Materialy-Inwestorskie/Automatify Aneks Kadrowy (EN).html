5
How to read this
Three things without which the figures above are easy to misread.
A country enters with a full eight-person team from day one.

Local staffing is always whole people: four managers, an admin, a delivery lead, customer success and ops — regardless of how many processes the country already runs. That is why 2028 costs almost six times 2027 on not quite four times the headcount: the difference is new countries, each starting with a complete team.

Countries are planned in people, the central function in cost.

In the countries we count positions, so staffing comes out in whole people. The central function is planned as four bucket amounts — R&D, board, G&A, marketing and sales — which is why central headcount can be fractional. For the individual central positions, look to the named lists rather than to the budget.

Operating leverage is real, but these two years do not yet show it in the numbers.

The same delivery lead handled 26 processes in Poland in 2027 and 260 processes in 2028 — cost unchanged, so every additional zloty of revenue in those years drops straight to the bottom line. The other half must be said at once: leverage measured over these two years is flattering, because staffing stands where the work is not yet. Once volume catches up with staffing — which in Poland happens in 2029, where delivery leads go to three and customer success to five — cost starts rising in whole-person steps. The right conclusion is not “headcount does not grow” but “headcount does not grow yet”.

Scope of this document. All figures come from the Automatify consolidated financial model in the no-process-expansion variant, as of 2026-08-29. Amounts are wages: they exclude ESOP, office overhead, recruitment and customer acquisition cost, which the budget carries separately.
